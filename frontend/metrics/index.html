How to read this page
  • False-positive rate (FPR) on "no technique": of the texts that human annotators labelled as ordinary information, the share the system flagged with some technique. Lower is better. This is the number that tells you how often an honest headline gets wrongly accused.
  • Precision of a technique: of the texts the system labelled with it, the share that really have it. Recall: of the texts that really have it, the share the system found.
  • Wilson interval: the 95% range where the true value plausibly lies given n. Small n gives wide intervals: do not over-read differences whose intervals overlap.
  • Confidence (High, Moderate, Disputed) is how strongly the system agrees with itself; it is not a probability. The calibration table checks whether "High" really is more accurate than "Moderate". See Methodology below.
  • Disputed means the system could not settle on an answer; such texts are never shown as a confident verdict.

Known limitations

  • Domain shift: public datasets label sentences from full news articles or memes, while the game uses headlines.
  • Public datasets may have leaked into the training data of the models, which can inflate their scores.
  • Techniques outside our six (for example flag-waving, slogans, repetition) are excluded from evaluation rather than counted as "no technique".
  • Small samples: intervals are wide, especially per technique.

Methodology

The classifier is an ensemble of three different large language models from different vendors. Each model labels the text independently with the same prompt; the reference answer is the majority vote. Confidence is the agreement level: High confidence when all three agree, Moderate confidence when two of three agree, and Disputed when no two agree. Agreement is not a probability, and models from the same era can share the same blind spots, so agreement can be confidently wrong.

Flagging policies: by default a text is flagged when at least two of three models name a technique; the stricter policy needs all three.

Numbers are produced by the eval script from a frozen test set and rendered from its results file; the page does not run the models on each visit.
